What this comparison tests

The workspace shell reserves a 232 px left rail and a 204 px right rail at its widest, so the writing column loses 436 px before the page has drawn anything. The reserved width is fixed while the window is not, so the two breakpoints create two sudden losses of usable content: crossing below xl (1200 px) gives the main column 203 px more, and crossing below lg (992 px) gives it another 231 px. A narrower window is not consistently narrower content — at 1200 px the page holds 704 px, while at 1199 px it holds 907 px.

This is a static mock of the real shell markup and the built values in _application_custom.scss, not a running Rails page. The content, counts, and names are illustrative; the widths, paddings, row heights, top bar, drawers, and tools entries follow the application. The review bar switches the design, the simulated viewport, both themes, typical or long scope names, an empty or populated pending panel, and the expanded or collapsed tools rail. The application view and stylesheet are untouched.

The four designs

  • Current. Left rail at lg, right rail at xl; below those widths each becomes a labelled drawer button in the workspace bar.
  • Compact rail. Keep the right rail at xl but make it 9rem (144 px), a 60 px saving. The same entries stay, but “Review requests” wraps and the tools scope loses a third of its label column.
  • Collapsible rail. Keep the full rail by default and add a Collapse tools button. Collapsed, it is a 48 px icon strip with title and accessible-name labels, and the writing column gains 156 px. The reader chooses it; the default still costs 204 px.
  • Tools from 1400. The right rail is a drawer below Bootstrap's xxl (1400 px) and a rail at and above it. The Tools button remains in the workspace bar, and the top bar's draft indicator still states pending work because the navbar stays expanded at these widths.

Recommended

Let the tools rail return at xxl: a drawer from 992 to 1399 and a rail at 1400 and above. Only the 1200–1399 range actually changes; below 1200 it is already a drawer. This is the smallest change that addresses the actual cliff: the current right rail costs 204 px exactly where the writing column is most constrained, and making it a drawer there returns 196–204 px at 1200–1280. At 1399 the page is already near its 1040 px reading maximum, so the same move shows only 77 px of content, though the main column is still wider. The Tools button is already the pattern below 1200, the top bar's search and draft indicator remain visible because the navbar is still expanded at these widths, and the rail returns at 1400 where the shell has room for it. The cost is one labelled click to reach Configuration and Collaboration between 1200 and 1399, and the 76 px step at the new boundary.

  • Keep the left rail at lg. The comparison did not find a reason to move it; the 231 px cliff at 992 is the same problem on the other side, and moving that boundary would cost the content navigation its persistent home on tablets. It is recorded here for a later decision, not folded into this recommendation.
  • Keep the pending panel and the top bar indicator. At 1200–1399 the indicator still answers “is anything waiting?” without opening anything, which is the requirement this item sets. Do not move pending work into the tools drawer.
  • Do not collapse the rail automatically. A collapsible rail is a reasonable reader preference at 1400 and above, where it gains 36 px at 1440 and 76 px at 1400, but it is not the fix for a constrained width: the default still takes 204 px, and the collapsed state hides every visible tool label.

Rejected, and why

  • Compact rail as the answer. It recovers 60 px at 1200 and 1280, and only 36 px at 1440 because the page is already at its reading maximum. “Review requests” wraps in a 144 px rail, its content grows to 755 px in a 750 px column at 1200 and 1280 and starts scrolling, and the 203 px cliff at 1200 remains. A narrower label column is a real cost for a gain too small to change the experience.
  • Collapsible rail as the default fix. The measured gain is real — 860 px of content at 1200 instead of 704 — but it depends on the reader finding and using a control, and the collapsed state is an icon strip whose labels are available on hover as a title or to assistive technology, but not in the visible reading surface. It is a good optional preference, not a substitute for not reserving the rail when there is no room.
  • Moving the left rail off lg. It would flatten the 992 cliff, but universe and story navigation would become a drawer on tablets and small laptops, and the item's first concern is the writing column at the xl boundary. Keep it as a separate decision.

What 200% zoom changes

At 200% zoom on a 1440 × 900 screen the CSS viewport is 720 × 450. The horizontal result is close to the 1200 px desktop case — 684 px of content, 20 px less — but the vertical result is not: the tools drawer body is 395 px tall for 737 px of content, and with two pending rows the panel runs from 328 px to 511 px, so its lower rows are below the fold until the drawer is scrolled. The left drawer is worse, at 874 px of content. No width overflows, and long names wrap rather than clip.

There is a separate finding here: below lg the draft indicator sat inside the collapsed navbar, so at 200% zoom it was not visible on first paint at all. That was true independently of which tools design is chosen, and at 1200–1399 the recommended design keeps the desktop indicator visible. The same change now renders the shared indicator beside the toggler below lg, so the count is on the first paint at 200% zoom too; test/system/navbar_layout_test.rb covers it.

Still to check outside this page

This comparison does not replace rendered browser checks, keyboard and screen-reader inspection, or participant testing. Before accepting a direction, confirm the drawer toggle's focus order and aria-expanded state, the Tools button's placement at exactly 1400, the rail's scroll position after a route change, and the page at real 200% zoom in both themes. The mock's numbers are layout measurements, not a claim that every page has the same content width: wide tables, the appearance grid, and the scene matrix each have their own horizontal behavior.
